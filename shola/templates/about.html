Share Your Language

SHOLA means share your language.

Ghanaian languages are missing from the tools people use every day. Not because nobody speaks them, but because too little of them has been written down.

Why this is needed

Machine translation gave us three ways to say each of {{ stats.words | thousands }} everyday English words in Twi, Ewe, Ga and Dagbani. Much of it is good. Some of it is word-for-word and no speaker would say it. Some of it is wrong.

Software cannot tell the good from the bad. A speaker can, in a couple of seconds. That is all SHOLA asks of you.

How a translation is confirmed

You see one word at a time

Each comes with three possible translations in your language.

You choose, skip or correct

Tap the wording you would use. If none of them fit, type your own — it carries the same weight as the options offered.

Other speakers see the same word

You are never the only person asked, so no single answer decides it on its own.

Agreement confirms it

When two speakers choose the same wording, that translation is confirmed. If everyone differs, the word stays open for more answers.

When you type your own translation, each one is a single tap. You can also hold down a plain letter such as e or o to reach its variants.

What we ask

About 1,000 words over a year, on the days you choose — a handful each day. If you miss a day, nothing is lost. Those words come back in your next email.

Your details

We use your email to send your daily list, and for nothing else. Your name and photo appear on the champions leaderboard beside the number of words you have checked. That is the only place they are shown, and the photo is optional.
