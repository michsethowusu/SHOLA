Share Your Language

SHOLA means share your language.

Ghanaian languages are missing from the tools people use every day. Not because nobody speaks them — because nobody has written down enough of them in a form a machine can learn from.

The problem with machine translation

We asked a language model to translate {{ stats.words | thousands }} everyday English nouns into Twi, Ewe, Ga and Dagbani, three ways each. It produced something for every word. Plenty of it is good. Some of it is a word-for-word calque no speaker would use, and some of it is simply wrong.

A machine cannot tell the difference. A speaker can, in about two seconds per word. That is the entire idea behind SHOLA.

How a word becomes an answer

Three guesses go out to speakers

Each word is shown with the three machine translations for your language.

You pick, skip, or correct

Tap the wording you would use. If none fit, type your own — your wording counts exactly as much as the machine's.

The same word goes to other speakers

Every word reaches one volunteer before any word reaches a second, so coverage comes first and agreement builds after.

Agreement decides

When two or more speakers land on the same wording, that becomes the recorded translation. If speakers disagree, the word stays open.

What we ask of you

A thousand words over a year, on the days you choose. That is a handful of words per day. Miss a day and nothing is lost: the words come back in your next email, spread over your remaining days so you still finish.

Your details

Your email is used to send your daily list, and for nothing else. Your name and photo appear on the champions leaderboard alongside your count — that is the only place they are shown, and the photo is optional.
